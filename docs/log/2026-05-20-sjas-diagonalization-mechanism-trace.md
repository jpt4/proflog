# SJAS Diagonalization Mechanism Trace

Date: 2026-05-20

This note follows up the diagonalization-efficiency question by locating the
exact Willard passages where the additive/multiplicative expressivity gap is
used, and where the semantic-tableaux diagonalization proof is said to fail
when multiplication is no longer total.

## Growth Illustration Reference

The clearest version is in Willard's 2011 arXiv paper, Example 3.7. It compares
two recursively defined sequences:

```text
x_i = x_{i-1} + x_{i-1}
y_i = y_{i-1} * y_{i-1}
```

The paper then states that the additive sequence yields `x_n = 2^(n+1)` while
the multiplicative/squaring sequence yields `y_n = 2^(2^n)`. The relevant
point is not only that `y_n` is numerically larger; it is that `y_n` has a
binary representation of length `2^n` despite being generated by roughly `O(n)`
defining steps. Willard explicitly uses this difference as the motivating
example for why Type-A systems can evade the semantic-tableaux second
incompleteness effect while Type-M systems cannot.

In the paper's own setup, the equations are represented by sentence sequences
`phi_0, phi_1, ... phi_n` and `psi_0, psi_1, ... psi_n`; those sequences imply,
or prove in the informal explanatory sense, the existence/value of `x_n` and
`y_n`. The multiplicative primitive therefore provides a short derivation of a
number whose binary code is exponentially larger than the derivation, while
the additive primitive does not.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2011_self_justifying_logics_arxiv_1108.6330.txt`,
  lines 560-607.

The 2013/2014 analytic-tableaux paper repeats the same intuition: the
exponential growth in `y_0, y_1, ...` enables the relevant self-reference, while
Type-A systems can prove their own tableaux consistency after suppressing
multiplication totality.

Local references:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2013_significance_self_justifying_axiom_systems_arxiv_1307.0150.txt`,
  lines 684-696.
- The same paper's floating-point/rounding discussion at lines 724-730 says
  the compatible operation avoids the bit-length-doubling behavior of integer
  multiplication by squaring.

## Exact Negative-Proof Step That Uses Multiplication

In the 2002 JSL paper on extending the semantic-tableaux version of the second
incompleteness theorem, the critical step is Lemma 4.7.

Lemma 4.7 proves that, assuming total multiplication in `Q + V`, there is a
semantic-tableaux proof `t` of a logarithmic-existence sentence with proof
length bounded by `O([LogLog(z)]^C)`. The proof does this by constructing a
top proof fragment `x_1` containing a squaring chain:

```text
u_0 = 2
u_{i+1} = (u_i)^2
z < u_n <= z^2
```

The second fragment `x_0` uses `u_n > z` to contradict the root sentence. Thus
the total multiplication primitive supplies a very short proof fragment whose
witness grows fast enough to dominate `z`.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2002_semantic_tableaux_robinson_q_author_jsl2.txt`,
  lines 781-817.

Lemma 4.8 then reuses the `x_0` and `x_1` fragments from Lemma 4.7 inside the
diagonal proof. Its constructed proof of `0 = 1` is divided into fragments
`x_0` through `x_4`; `x_0` and `x_1` are inherited from Lemma 4.7, and the
remaining fragments are kept "z-tiny" by the hypotheses around `Paradox`,
`Subst`, and `SemPrf`.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2002_semantic_tableaux_robinson_q_author_jsl2.txt`,
  lines 859-997 and 1086-1128.

The exact collapse point is the paper's final paragraph: when multiplication is
changed from total function to three-place relation, Lemma 4.7 is no longer
valid; because Lemma 4.8 needs Lemma 4.7's `x_0` and `x_1` fragments, the rest
of the proof collapses.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2002_semantic_tableaux_robinson_q_author_jsl2.txt`,
  lines 1678-1683.

The earlier 2000 conference version states the same point with Lemma 8 and
Lemma 9 instead of Lemma 4.7 and Lemma 4.8:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2000_tableaux_robinson_q_author_tab2000.txt`,
  lines 573-581 and 849-867.

## Positive Type-A Mechanism

The later 2011 paper recasts the successful Type-A/tableaux side through a
stability invariant. Fact D.3 says that for a `Normed(a,b)` extension of the
U-Grounding base system, any sufficiently small `Z`-based deduction tree must
have at least one root-to-leaf branch that is not closed. The proof rests on
the U-height of the deduction tree: with only U-Grounding functions, a small
Godel-numbered deduction tree cannot contain enough U-Grounding term growth to
close every branch.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2011_self_justifying_logics_arxiv_1108.6330.txt`,
  lines 2176-2229.

Willard then says this invariant would collapse if a multiplication function
symbol were added. Footnote 26 gives the reason: the `y_i = y_{i-1} * y_{i-1}`
growth lets one build tiny `Z`-based deduction trees with Godel numbers below
the `(a/b)^4` threshold while still closing every branch.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2011_self_justifying_logics_arxiv_1108.6330.txt`,
  lines 2237-2240 and 2276-2286.

Theorem D.4 uses Fact D.3 to prove the `xi*` Type-A configuration stable. If a
bad small proof existed, the proof can be viewed as a `Z`-based deduction tree;
Fact D.3 then supplies an unclosed branch, contradicting its being a semantic
tableaux proof. The resulting remark says multiplication totality is the
trigger point that makes the semantic-tableaux second incompleteness theorem
active, while Type-A systems retain addition but suppress multiplication
totality.

Local reference:

- `target/sjas-pdf-text/willard2011_self_justifying_logics_arxiv_1108.6330.txt`,
  lines 2237-2274 and 2378-2389.

## Mechanism Summary

The mechanism is therefore not merely:

```text
the system lacks total multiplication, so diagonalization is impossible
```

and it is not exactly:

```text
the Godel number #G cannot be named at all
```

The sharper mechanism is:

1. The semantic-tableaux second-incompleteness proof needs a small closed proof
   object generated from the diagonal sentence.
2. With total multiplication, the proof can manufacture a short squaring chain
   whose final witness dominates the relevant proof-code bound. This is Lemma
   4.7/Lemma 8.
3. Lemma 4.8/Lemma 9 reuses that short squaring proof fragment to turn a
   diagonal `Paradox` tuple into a small proof of `0 = 1`.
4. If multiplication is only a relation and not a total function, the short
   squaring-chain lemma is unavailable.
5. On the positive Type-A side, Fact D.3 supplies the replacement invariant:
   sufficiently small U-Grounding deduction trees must retain an unclosed
   branch.
6. That invariant directly blocks the attempted closed tableau needed by the
   destructive diagonal argument.

So the "arithmetic efficiency" reading is substantially right if expressed as
proof-certificate compression. Total multiplication gives enough term-growth
efficiency to build a tiny closed proof object whose witnesses outgrow its
description. Type-A U-Grounding with semantic tableaux lacks that compression,
and the U-height/Normed/Good invariant forces any sufficiently small attempted
closed proof tree to leave a branch open.

## Consequence For Word/Tree Object Languages

For a word-native or tree-native decidable SJAS candidate, arithmetization is
not the only issue. The candidate must still answer the same efficiency
question for its chosen proof apparatus:

- Does `D` admit compact closed proof certificates that can diagonalize against
  the proof predicate?
- Or does the object language have a Fact-D.3-like invariant ensuring that all
  sufficiently small proof certificates leave an open branch or fail a local
  validity condition?

Without such an invariant, replacing numbers by words or trees may only move
the diagonalization mechanism into a different coding discipline.
